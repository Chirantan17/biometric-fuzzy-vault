The Dilemma & The Solution

1. The Avalanche Effect Failure

Standard cryptographic hashes (SHA-256) require 100% exact bit matching. Changing a single character flips 50% of hash bits. Because physical fingerprint scans naturally fluctuate on every touch due to pressure, angle, and moisture, standard password hashing is mathematically unusable for biometrics.

# Standard SHA-256 Behavior
Scan 1: SHA256("Scan_45.2_12.1") ➔ a665a45920...
Scan 2: SHA256("Scan_45.4_12.0") ➔ 3f79a90baa... (Failed)

2. Non-Revocability Hazard

To bypass hash mismatches, naive systems store raw minutiae coordinate templates in database files. When a database is breached, the user's biological template is compromised permanently—unlike alphanumeric passwords, a human cannot be issued a replacement fingerprint.

Identity Burn Threat
Biometric templates are non-revocable. Storing raw coordinates creates irrecoverable identity theft.
Phase 01

Secret Binding

The engine embeds the secret key $S$ into polynomial $p(x) = c_0 + c_1 x + c_2 x^2 + c_3 x^3 \pmod{2^{31}-1}$.

Phase 02

Minutiae Locking

12–16 genuine coordinates extracted from the scan are evaluated directly on the curve: $(x_i, p(x_i))$.

Phase 03

Chaff Cloaking

140+ decoy noise points $(x'_j, y'_j)$ with $y'_j \neq p(x'_j)$ are uniformly injected to camouflage the real points.

Phase 04

Lagrange & CRC

A genuine scan matching $\ge 4$ points reconstructs $p(x)$. An embedded 16-bit CRC checksum guarantees 0.0% False Accepts.
