The Biometric Security Dilemma

Why standard hashing algorithms like SHA-256 completely fail on physical biometric data.

1. The Avalanche Effect (Passwords)

When you enter Secret123, SHA-256 generates a deterministic hash. If you change a single character to Secret124, over 50% of the output bits flip completely. This strict exactness makes traditional hashes ideal for text, but impossible for human biology.

SHA256("Secret123"): a665a45920422f9d4...
SHA256("Secret124"): 3f79a90baab5d301c... (50% Mismatch)
2. The Non-Revocability Hazard

Every time you touch a sensor, variations in finger pressure, sweat, or tilt alter the scanned minutiae coordinates. Because standard hashes cannot tolerate noise, legacy systems often store raw biometric images directly in databases. If that server is breached, your biometric identity is permanently stolen.

You can change a compromised password in 10 seconds. You cannot change your biological fingerprint.

The 4-Step Mathematical Solution

How the Juels-Sudan scheme locks secret keys inside noise without storing raw templates.

Step 1

The Secret Curve

A secret key $S$ is embedded into the baseline coefficient $c_0$ of a polynomial curve $p(x)$ evaluated over the finite field $\mathbb{F}_{2^{31}-1}$.

Step 2

Planting True Pins

12–16 genuine minutiae coordinates extracted from the fingerprint scan are evaluated directly on the polynomial curve: $(x_i, p(x_i))$.

Step 3

Camouflage Swarm

140+ random decoy coordinates (chaff points) are scattered across the plane. These fake points do not lie on the secret polynomial curve.

Step 4

Lagrange & CRC

A genuine user matching $\ge 4$ points redraws the exact curve using Lagrange interpolation. Embedded CRC-16 checksums reject imposter curves.
