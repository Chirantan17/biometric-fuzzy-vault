Theoretical Bottleneck

The Biometric Security Dilemma

Why traditional exact-match cryptographic algorithms cannot secure human biology.

1. The Avalanche Effect Barrier

Standard cryptographic hash functions like SHA-256 exhibit extreme bit diffusion. Changing a single character in a password alters over 50% of the output bits. Because biometric sensors produce micro-deviations on every single touch due to skin elasticity and angle, standard cryptographic hashing fails 100% of the time.

# Deterministic Hash Evaluation
Scan A: SHA256("Print_Coord_X45") ➔ c8a2ef...
Scan B: SHA256("Print_Coord_X46") ➔ 14b901... (Mismatch)

2. The Non-Revocability Hazard

To bypass the avalanche dilemma, naive architectures store raw minutiae coordinates or unencrypted template files on backend databases. When a database breach occurs, an individual's biological identity is permanently compromised—you cannot issue a new fingerprint like you issue a new alphanumeric password.

Zero-Tolerance Threat
Biometric traits are unchangeable. Storage of plaintext templates is critical security negligence.

Cryptographic Mechanism

The 4-Step Sensor-to-Cipher Flow

How the Juels-Sudan scheme locks secret keys in mathematical noise without saving raw biometrics.

Phase 01

Polynomial Binding

The engine constructs a secret cubic polynomial $p(x) = c_0 + c_1 x + c_2 x^2 + c_3 x^3$ over $\mathbb{F}_{2^{31}-1}$. The constant $c_0$ embeds the master cryptographic key.

Phase 02

Minutiae Evaluation

12–16 genuine biometric coordinates $(x_i)$ extracted from the physical scan are evaluated directly on the polynomial curve: $(x_i, p(x_i))$.

Phase 03

Chaff Injection

140+ decoy coordinates $(x'_j, y'_j)$ with $y'_j \neq p(x'_j)$ are uniformly scattered across the plane, completely obscuring genuine points from attackers.

Phase 04

Lagrange & CRC

A genuine user matching $\ge 4$ points redraws the exact polynomial via Lagrange interpolation. Embedded 16-bit CRC checksums immediately reject imposter curves.
