The Dilemma & The Solution

1. The Avalanche Effect Failure

Cryptographic hash functions like SHA-256 require bit-exact inputs. A 1-pixel shift flips 50% of the output bits. Because biometric scans naturally vary due to pressure, angle, and sweat, standard hashing fails 100% of the time.

Scan 1: SHA256("Coord_45") ➔ c8a2ef...
Scan 2: SHA256("Coord_46") ➔ 14b901... (Mismatch)

2. Non-Revocability Hazard

Naive systems store raw minutiae templates in server databases. When that database is breached, the user's biological template is permanently stolen. You can change a password, but you cannot replace a biological fingerprint.

Zero-Tolerance Threat: Biological identity cannot be re-issued upon server leakage.
